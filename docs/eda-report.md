## 4. Decision-relevant observations

1. **Class imbalance is severe (~15.5% positive).** A model or threshold policy optimized for accuracy would default toward predicting no-purchase for almost every session; PR-AUC and an explicit business-cost threshold (Section 5.4 of the blueprint) are required instead of accuracy.
2. **Returning visitors convert at 13.93% vs. new visitors at 24.91%.** Visitor type alone is a weak but non-trivial signal; it is a legitimate, decision-time-available feature (no leakage risk).
3. **Engagement depth separates converters from non-converters:** sessions that convert spend 1876s average on product-related pages vs. 1070s for sessions that do not convert, and converters have a lower average bounce rate (0.0051 vs. 0.0253 overall mean 0.0222). This supports including engagement-duration and bounce/exit-rate features in the deployment-safe feature set.
4. **Conversion rate varies by month** (highest: Nov at 25.35%; lowest: Feb at 1.63%), and the dataset has no real timestamp — only a categorical month. This is a known limitation: seasonal effects exist but cannot be modeled as a true temporal holdout (see blueprint Section 4.5).
5. **Weekend sessions convert at 17.40% vs. weekday sessions at 14.89%** — a small, plausible difference worth keeping as a feature but not worth over-interpreting given the class imbalance.
6. **`TrafficType` and `Browser`/`OperatingSystems`/`Region` are anonymized integer codes with no documented real-world mapping.** Per decision D06, this project treats `TrafficType` strictly as an anonymous channel/source proxy and does not claim to measure or causally test platform dependence from it.
